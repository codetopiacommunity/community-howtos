# Your first pull request, in your browser

In this guide you add one small file about yourself to our practice
project on GitHub, and someone from our team accepts it. It is the same
thing developers do every day, and it is how you take part in open
source projects.

- **Time:** about fifteen minutes, then a wait while we look at it.
- **What you need:** a computer or laptop, and an internet connection.
  Nothing to install. You do everything in your web browser (Chrome,
  Firefox, Safari, Edge or similar).
- **You cannot break anything.** It is a practice project, made for
  this.

> [!TIP]
> **This guide is long, on purpose.** We wrote down every click and
> every screen, so you never have to guess. Most steps take less than a
> minute. Take your time. Slow is fine.

**We are a community of learners.** Everyone here started as a
beginner, and not knowing something is simply where learning starts.
If you get stuck, ask in <Channel>ask-for-help</Channel>: your question
helps the next person who gets stuck in the same place. And once you
have done this, you can help the next beginner too.

> [!NOTE]
> **On a phone?** It works, but a computer is easier. On a small screen
> a few buttons are in different places: see
> [On a small screen](#on-a-small-screen) before you start.

---

## How to use this guide

- **Do one step at a time.** Each step ends with *You will know it
  worked when...*. Check that before you move on.
- **Keep this guide open.** Links to GitHub open in a new browser tab.
  Switch between the tabs at the top of your browser: this guide in
  one, GitHub in the other.
- **The pictures** show a test account called *cerebrocerberus*. You
  will see your own name in those places. The red boxes show what to
  click.

## Words you will see

You do not need to remember these. Come back here when a word is new.

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **GitHub** | A website where people keep projects and work on them together. |
| **Repository** (repo) | A project on GitHub: a set of folders and files. |
| **Folder** | A group of files, like a folder on your computer. |
| **File** | One document inside the project. Yours will be a short text file. |
| **`.md`** | The end of a file name for a simple text file. *md* stands for Markdown, a way of writing text with headings and lists. |
| **Fork** | Your own copy of someone else's project, on your GitHub account. Changing it cannot change the original. |
| **Commit** | A saved change, with a short note saying what you changed. |
| **Branch** | One line of saved changes in a project. Every project has a main one, called **main**. You will only use **main**. |
| **Pull request** | A message to the project's owners: "here is my change, please take it into your project." |
| **Review** | When someone from the project looks at your pull request. |
| **Merge** | When they accept your pull request, so your change becomes part of their project. |

**The plan in one sentence:** you make your own copy of our project, add
your file to your copy, then ask us to take it into ours.

## Before you start: your GitHub account

There are three short parts here: **A**, **B** and **C**. Do them in
order.

### A. Have a GitHub account

**If you do not have a GitHub account yet, or you are not sure:**
create one now. It is free.

<a href="https://github.com/signup" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Create a GitHub account</a>

GitHub asks you its questions one screen at a time. Answer them and
follow what the screen says. Two tips:

- Use an **email address you can open right now**. GitHub sends you an
  email to check that it is yours.
- Choose a **username** you are happy for others to see. It shows next
  to everything you do on GitHub.

Stuck while creating your account? Ask in <Channel>ask-for-help</Channel>.

**If you already have a GitHub account:** you have nothing to do here.
Go on to part B.

### B. Sign in to GitHub

1. Open <a href="https://github.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">github.com</a>.
   It opens in a new tab.
2. Look at the **top right corner** of the page. You will see one of two
   things. Find the one that matches your screen.

**What you might see: two buttons with the words Sign in and Sign up.**

![The top right corner of GitHub when you are not signed in, with the Sign in and Sign up buttons](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/00a-signed-out.jpg)

This means you are **not signed in yet**. Do this:

1. Click <Button>Sign in</Button>.
2. A page called **Sign in to GitHub** opens.
3. Click inside the **Username or email address** box. Type the
   username or the email address you used when you created your GitHub
   account.
4. Click inside the **Password** box. Type your GitHub password.
5. Click the green <Button>Sign in</Button> button.

![The Sign in to GitHub page, with the two boxes and the Sign in button](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/00c-sign-in-page.jpg)

6. If GitHub then asks you for a code, it has sent one to you. Look in
   your email (or your text messages), type the code in, and continue.
7. Look at the top right corner again. It now looks like the next
   picture.

Forgot your password? On the **Sign in to GitHub** page, click
**Forgot password?** and follow the steps.

**What you might see: a small circle at the far right, and no Sign in
button.**

![The top right corner of GitHub when you are signed in, with your profile picture circle at the far right](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/00b-signed-in.jpg)

This means you **are already signed in**. You have nothing to do here.

The circle is your **profile picture**. If you never added a photo,
it shows a coloured pattern that GitHub made for you, like the one in
the picture.

**You will know part B worked when** the top right corner shows the
small circle, and there is no **Sign in** button.

### C. Find your username

1. Click your **profile picture**: the small circle at the top right.
2. A menu opens. Your **username** is the name at the very top of it.

![The menu under your profile picture, with the username at the top](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/01-username.jpg)

3. Write it down exactly as it is: the same small and capital letters,
   and any dashes (`-`). You need it in Step 3.
4. Close the menu: click anywhere else on the page.

**You will know part C worked when** you have your username written
down.

## Step 1: Make your own copy (fork)

You cannot add files to our project directly. Instead, you make your
own copy of it on your account, called a **fork**, and add your file
there. You only ever need one copy.

1. Open our practice project:
   <a href="https://github.com/codetopiacommunity/open-source-practice" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">open-source-practice</a>.
   At the top left it says **codetopiacommunity / open-source-practice**.
2. At the top right, find the button with the word **Fork** and a
   number. Click the word <Button>Fork</Button> itself, not the small
   arrow next to it.

   ![The practice project, with the Fork button at the top right](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/02-fork-button.jpg)

3. A page called **Create a new fork** opens. Your username is already
   in the **Owner** box, and **open-source-practice** in the
   **Repository name** box. **Do not change anything.** Click the green
   <Button>Create fork</Button> button at the bottom right.

   ![The Create a new fork page, with the Create fork button](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/03-create-fork.jpg)

4. You see *Forking codetopiacommunity/open-source-practice. It should
   only take a few seconds.* Wait. The page changes on its own.

**You will know it worked when** you see the words *forked from
codetopiacommunity/open-source-practice* under the big title
**open-source-practice**. On a computer, the top left of the page also
shows **your username / open-source-practice**. This is **your copy**.

![Your copy, with your username at the top left and "forked from" under the title](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/04-your-copy.jpg)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Do Steps 2, 3 and 4 in **your copy**: the one that says *forked from
> codetopiacommunity/open-source-practice* under the title. If you ever
> lose it, click your profile picture, then **Repositories**, then
> **open-source-practice**.

## Step 2: Open the contributors folder

1. In your copy, you see a list of folders and files. Click the folder
   called **contributors** (it has a small folder icon).
2. The folder opens. You see a file called `README.md`, and one file for
   every person who has done this before you.
3. At the top right, click <Button>Add file</Button>. A small menu opens
   with **Create new file** and **Upload files**.

   ![The contributors folder in your copy, with the Add file button](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/05-add-file.jpg)

   On a small screen there is no **Add file** button. Tap the **⋯**
   button at the top right instead (see
   [On a small screen](#on-a-small-screen)).

4. Click <Button>Create new file</Button>.

   ![The Add file menu, with Create new file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/06-create-new-file.jpg)

**You will know it worked when** you see a page with a small box that
says **Name your file...** at the top, and a big empty box below it
that says **Enter file contents here**.

## Step 3: Write your file

### Name it

1. Click inside the small **Name your file...** box.
2. Type your **username**, then `.md`, with no spaces. If your username
   is *kofi-mensah*, type:

   ```
   kofi-mensah.md
   ```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Use your GitHub username, not your real name. Two people can have
> the same name, but never the same username.

### Write in it

1. Copy the text in the box below. Click the **copy icon** at the top
   right of the box: it turns into a tick ✓ when it is copied.

   ```
   # Your Name

   - From: Your city, your country
   - Learning right now: what you are learning
   - Fun fact: one thing about you
   ```

2. Go back to GitHub. Click inside the big box that says **Enter file
   contents here**.
3. Paste: press <Key>Ctrl</Key> and <Key>V</Key> together. On a Mac,
   press <Key>Cmd</Key> and <Key>V</Key>. Or right-click inside the box
   and choose **Paste**.
4. Now change the words to your own. Replace *Your Name*, *Your city,
   your country*, *what you are learning* and *one thing about you*.
   Leave everything else exactly as it is:
   - Keep the `#` and the space after it at the start of the first
     line. It makes your name a heading.
   - Keep the `-` and the space after it at the start of the other
     lines. They make a list.

For example, Ama from Kumasi would end up with:

```
# Ama Owusu

- From: Kumasi, Ghana
- Learning right now: HTML and CSS
- Fun fact: I can solve a Rubik's cube
```

> [!TIP]
> If you press <Key>Enter</Key> at the end of a line that starts with
> `-`, GitHub starts a new line with a `-` for you. If you see an extra
> `-` on its own line at the end, delete it.

![The new file, with the name box filled in and the text written, and the Commit changes button](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/07-name-and-write.jpg)

**You will know it worked when** the name box shows your username
ending in `.md`, and the big box shows your own five lines (numbered 1
to 5 on the left).

## Step 4: Save it (commit)

There are two green buttons with almost the same name. The first one
(with three dots) opens a box. The second one (without dots) saves.

1. Click the green <Button>Commit changes...</Button> button at the top
   right of the page. A box called **Commit changes** opens in the
   middle of the page.
2. The **Commit message** box already has some text, like *Create
   kofi-mensah.md*. Replace it with a short note of what you did:
   - Click inside the box.
   - Press <Key>Ctrl</Key> and <Key>A</Key> together (<Key>Cmd</Key>
     and <Key>A</Key> on a Mac) to select the old text.
   - Type `Add kofi-mensah to contributors`, with your own username.
3. Leave **Extended description** empty.
4. Leave **Commit directly to the main branch** selected. It only
   changes **your copy**, so it is safe.
5. Click the green <Button>Commit changes</Button> button at the bottom
   right of the box.

![The Commit changes box, with the Commit message box and the Commit changes button](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/08-commit-dialog.jpg)

**You will know it worked when** you are back in the contributors folder
and your file is in the list. Near the top, GitHub says *This branch is
1 commit ahead of codetopiacommunity/open-source-practice:main*. That
means: your copy has one change that our project does not have yet.

## Step 5: Ask us to take it (pull request)

1. On the same line as *1 commit ahead*, click the
   <Button>Contribute</Button> button. A box opens.
2. In the box, click the green <Button>Open pull request</Button>
   button.

   ![The Contribute box, with the Open pull request button](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/09-contribute.jpg)

3. A page called **Comparing changes** opens. You do not need to change
   anything on it:
   - The grey row at the top shows from where to where: from your copy
     to ours. Leave it.
   - It says **Able to merge** with a green tick. Good.
   - **Add a title** is already filled in from your commit message.
   - **Add a description** is optional. You can leave it empty, or write
     a line like *My first pull request!*
4. Click the green <Button>Create pull request</Button> button under
   the description. Click the words, not the small arrow next to them.

   ![The pull request page, with the Create pull request button](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/10-create-pull-request.jpg)

You see a page with your title at the top and a green **Open** label
under it, and it says *your username wants to merge 1 commit into
codetopiacommunity:main*. That is your pull request. Well done.

</DoneWhen>

![Your pull request, with the green Open label](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/11-pull-request-open.jpg)

You do not need to post it anywhere: we can see it on GitHub.

**Your pull request's link** is the web address of this page. To copy
it: click the **address bar** at the very top of your browser (where the
address starting with `https://github.com/` is), then press
<Key>Ctrl</Key> and <Key>C</Key> together (<Key>Cmd</Key> and
<Key>C</Key> on a Mac). You only need it if you ask for help.

## What happens next

Someone from our Projects & Tech team looks at your pull request. You do
not need to do anything while you wait.

Each time they do something, GitHub sends you an email, and the
**inbox icon** at the top right of GitHub gets a small blue dot. Click
the inbox icon, then click the line about your pull request to open it.

![The inbox icon with a blue dot, and the notification about the pull request](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/13-notification.jpg)

**To find your pull request again** at any time: click the **pull
request icon** at the top right of any GitHub page (two dots joined by
lines). Your pull request is listed there.

![The pull request icon at the top right, and the pull request in the list](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/12-find-your-pull-request.jpg)

There are two things that can happen.

### If they ask for a change

That is normal, and not a mistake. It happens to experienced developers
every day. On your pull request page you see **requested changes** next
to a red icon, and under it their comment saying what to change.

![A review asking for a change, with the red requested changes icon](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/14-changes-requested.jpg)

To make the change:

1. Go to **your copy**: click your profile picture, then
   **Repositories**, then **open-source-practice**.
2. Click the **contributors** folder, then click **your file** (the one
   with your username).
3. Your file opens. Click the **pencil icon** at the top right of it.

   ![Your file in your copy, with the pencil icon](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/15-edit-your-file.jpg)

4. Your text appears in the big box again. Make the change they asked
   for.
5. Click the green <Button>Commit changes...</Button> button at the top
   right, then the green <Button>Commit changes</Button> button in the
   box. You can keep the commit message GitHub suggests.

That is all. You do **not** open a new pull request: your change is
added to the same one on its own. On your pull request page, your new
commit appears under their comment, and it now says *wants to merge 2
commits*.

![The pull request with the new commit added under the review](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/16-change-joined.jpg)

### When it is accepted (merged)

The green **Open** label turns into a purple **Merged** label, and the
page says **Pull request successfully merged and closed**.

![The pull request with the purple Merged label](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/17-merged.jpg)

Your file is now in
<a href="https://github.com/codetopiacommunity/open-source-practice/tree/main/contributors" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">our contributors folder</a>,
next to everyone else's. You made your first pull request. Tell us in
<Channel>wins-and-milestones</Channel>!

**Nobody has replied after three days?** Ask in
<Channel>ask-for-help</Channel> and paste your pull request's link.

## Coming back later

If you come back to your copy another day, it may say *This branch is
1 commit behind codetopiacommunity/open-source-practice:main* (or more
than 1). That means our project has changes your copy does not have yet.
It is normal. To bring your copy up to date:

1. Click the <Button>Sync fork</Button> button on the same line.

   ![Your copy saying it is behind, with the Sync fork button](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/18-copy-behind.jpg)

2. A box says **This branch is out-of-date**. Click the green
   <Button>Update branch</Button> button.

   ![The Sync fork box, with the Update branch button](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/19-update-branch.jpg)

**You will know it worked when** it says *This branch is up to date
with codetopiacommunity/open-source-practice:main*.

## On a small screen

We checked these steps in a narrow browser window, the size of a phone.
Three things are different from the pictures above. Everything else
works the same.

- **The top left does not show whose project it is.** It shows
  **··· / open-source-practice**. To tell your copy from ours, look
  under the big title: your copy says *forked from
  codetopiacommunity/open-source-practice*.
- **There is no Add file button.** In the contributors folder, tap the
  **⋯** button at the top right. The menu that opens has
  <Button>Create new file</Button> at the top. Tap that.
- **Do not tap Delete directory** in that menu (it is in red). It would

## Stuck?

- **There is no Fork or Add file button.** You are probably not signed
  in: if the top right shows **Sign in**, follow part B of *Before you
  start*. On a small screen, see also *On a small screen*.
- **GitHub says "You need to fork this repository to propose
  changes".** You are in our project, not your copy: the page does not
  say *forked from* under the title. Go to your copy (click your profile picture,
  then **Repositories**, then **open-source-practice**) and do the step
  there.
- **GitHub says "A file with the same name already exists".** You
  already added your file earlier. Click **Cancel** to close the box,
  then click **contributors** in the path at the top of the page (if
  your browser asks whether to leave the page, choose to leave). Your
  file is in the list. If you have not opened a pull request for
  it yet, carry on from Step 5.
- **You clicked Fork and GitHub says "No available destinations to
  fork this repository".** You already have a copy from before. Click
  the link under that message (*your username/open-source-practice*):
  that is your copy. Carry on from Step 2.

  ![The fork page saying there are no available destinations, with a link to the existing copy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codetopiacommunity/community-howtos/main/images/Contributing/first-pr/21-already-have-a-copy.jpg)

- **Your file shows** `# Your Name` **instead of your name.** You forgot
  to replace the example words. Use the pencil icon (see *If they ask
  for a change*) to fix it.
- **Something looks different from the pictures.** GitHub changes its
  pages from time to time. Ask in <Channel>ask-for-help</Channel>, and
  tell us, so we can fix this guide.
- **Anything else?** Ask in <Channel>ask-for-help</Channel>. Say which
  step you are on, what you clicked, and what you see. A screenshot
  helps.
