# Ways to Contribute

There are three ways to be here. Each one is complete on its own, and
nobody is behind for staying in the first.

> [!TIP]
> If you have not helped out yet, start with
> [Help out](./04-make-your-first-contribution) instead. It gives you
> one small task with a clear finish line. This page is the full menu,
> and a menu is easier to read once you have eaten here once.

|  | Take part | Help out | Run it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who** | Every member | Anyone, whenever they like | The core team |
| **Commitment** | None | One thing at a time | 3 to 5 hours a month, for 3 months |
| **Looks like** | Come to events, ask and answer, learn, build and write your own things | Finish one thing that helps the community | A named role with a monthly goal |
| **Needs a yes?** | No | No | Yes, from a team lead |
| **What you get** | Everything the community offers | The Contributor role and your name in the monthly recap | A core role, real experience, and a reference after a full term |

**Not sure which one you are doing? Ask who it is for.** If it is for
you (your learning, your project, your article), you are taking part.
If it is for the community (something the community needed done), you
are helping out. If it is your job every month, you are running it.

> [!NOTE]
> All roles in the community are voluntary and unpaid, including team
> leads. What you gain is experience, credit, and a public track record
> of your work.

## Take part

Most members spend all their time here, and that is a success, not a
failure. Taking part needs no permission, no role and no sign-up beyond
joining.

- **Show up.** Come to meetups, workshops, online sessions and
  hackathons.
- **Ask and answer.** Get unstuck in **#ask-for-help**, and help the
  next person when you can.
- **Build your own things.** Share what you are working on, get
  feedback, find collaborators.
- **Write and share.** Post your own articles, threads and projects.
  The community reads them and boosts the good ones.
- **Learn.** These guides explain how everything here works. For
  courses and mentorship, go to
  <a href="https://academy.codetopia.org" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Codetopia Academy</a>.
- **Find opportunities.** Jobs, gigs and introductions that come
  through the community.

## Help out

Finish one thing that helps the community. You do not need to ask
first. There are three ways in: a starter task, a task from the task
board, or your own idea.

🔗 [Help out](./04-make-your-first-contribution)

### On GitHub, anyone can help

Our repos are public. If you spot a bug, a typo, a broken link, or
something that no longer works the way the docs say it does, you can
report it or fix it yourself. You do not need to be a member.

🔗 [Your first fix](./05-your-first-fix)

Contributing on GitHub but not a member yet? Join us. You will be able
to talk directly with maintainers, get credit for your work inside the
community, and unlock everything else on this page. It takes a few
minutes.

🔗 [Join the community](../Getting-Started/01-join-the-community)

## Writing

Writing is open to every member. Nobody needs a role or a team to
write. What changes is whose name it goes out under, because that
decides whether anyone reviews it.

| You are writing | Where it goes | Who reviews it |
|---|---|---|
| Your own article | Your blog, LinkedIn, anywhere | Nobody. Share it in Discord and Comms & Brand may boost it |
| An article for Codetopia's blog | Codetopia's Hashnode, under your name | Comms & Brand edits and publishes |
| A howto | This repo | The Guides team reviews the pull request |
| Something a team needs (an event recap, an announcement) | Codetopia's blog or socials | Whoever posted the task |

**Howtos and articles are different jobs.** A howto unblocks someone:
how to join, how to use Discord, how to contribute to a project. An
article teaches: tutorials, deep dives, lessons learned. Ask yourself
who is reading it and why. If they are stuck and want it fixed, it is
a howto. If they are curious and want to understand, it is an article.

### Getting an article on Codetopia's blog

1. Write the draft anywhere, in a Google Doc or a Hashnode draft.
2. Send it to Comms & Brand in their Discord channel.
3. Comms replies: published, needs changes, or not a fit and why.
   No reply? Nudge them in the same channel, then ask the Comms &
   Brand lead.

Once it is published, it counts as a contribution and your name goes
in the monthly recap.

### Writing a howto

Write it through GitHub, the same way as code. Not comfortable with
GitHub yet? Write it wherever you like and post it in **#general**. A
member will open the pull request for you, with your name on it.

## Run it: the teams

The community is run by six teams. Each has one lead and a monthly
goal, and every team exists to give members something to take part in.

| Team | What it does | What members get from it |
|---|---|---|
| **Events** | Meetups, workshops, online sessions, hackathons | Events to attend |
| **Projects & Tech** | Community open source projects, the website, Discord bots, tooling | Real projects to learn on and contribute to |
| **Guides** | These howtos and the community docs | Guides that explain how the community works |
| **Comms & Brand** | Social media, design, announcements, the monthly recap | News, credit, and a boost for your own work |
| **Partnerships** | Sponsors, venues, schools, other communities | Venues, sponsors, opportunities, and links to other communities |
| **Operations** | The team roster, tools and access, the member survey | A community that runs smoothly, and a say in what comes next |

Supporting all six, the **Community Manager** looks after members:
Discord, welcoming newcomers, and being the person to go to with a
problem.

You join a team by being offered a role, not by clicking a button.
Open roles are posted on
<a href="https://community.codetopia.org/careers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Careers</a>,
and you apply by showing two things you have already finished for that
team.

🔗 [How the community works](./07-how-the-community-works) explains
the whole path, from your first task to leading a team.
