# Write for the blog

Learned something, built something, or went to an event worth telling
people about? Write about it for our blog.

You do not need to be an expert or a "good writer". Some of the most
useful articles are written by people who just figured something out:
*"How I fixed my first npm error"* helps the next beginner more than an
expert ever could.

About **ten minutes** to get set up. Writing the article takes as long as
you like.

## What to write about

Anything that would help or interest another member. For example:

- Something you learned, and how you learned it
- A project you built: what it does, and what went wrong on the way
- A problem you solved, step by step
- What happened at one of our events
- Your path into tech so far

Not sure if your idea fits? Ask before you write a whole article: post
it in <Channel>get-involved</Channel> with the **Blog** tag, in a line or
two.

## Step 1: Create a Hashnode account

*Already have one? Skip to Step 2.*

1. Open
   <a href="https://hashnode.com/onboard" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">hashnode.com/onboard</a>.
2. Sign up with Google, GitHub or your email. It is free.
3. Hashnode asks you to pick a **username**. Choose one you are happy for
   readers to see.

**Find your username:** it is shown on your Hashnode profile with an `@`
in front, like `@ama-dev`. You need it in Step 2.

## Step 2: Ask to join the blog

You only do this once.

1. Open <Channel>get-involved</Channel> on Discord and start a new post.
   New to forum posts?
   [Using Discord](../getting-started/using-discord#post-in-a-forum-channel)
   shows how.
2. Pick the **Blog** tag.
3. Write something like this, with your own username:

   ```
   I'd like to write for the blog. My Hashnode username is @yourname.
   ```

4. Send it.

An editor invites you to the blog as a **contributor**: a writer who can
send drafts to the editors.

## Step 4: Write your draft

1. On Hashnode, click <Button>Write</Button>. A new, empty article opens.
2. Add a **title**, then write your article underneath.

Until you send it, your article is a **draft**: it is saved as you go,
and only you can see it. You can close it and come back to it whenever
you like, from your drafts on Hashnode.

> [!TIP]
> Short is fine. A clear article of 500 words is better than a long one
> nobody finishes. Read it out loud once before you send it: you will
> catch most mistakes that way.

## Step 5: Send it to the editors

When your article is ready:

1. Open your draft and go to its **settings**.
2. Choose our blog from the list of blogs.
3. Click <Button>Submit for review</Button>.

## What happens next

An editor reads your draft. Then they do one of two things:

- **Publish it.** It goes live on
  <a href="https://BLOG-ADDRESS" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">our blog</a>
  under your name.
- **Ask for changes.** They tell you what to change. Make the changes in
  your draft, then send it again as in Step 5.

{/* DECIDE: where editors give feedback (comments on the draft in
    Hashnode, or a message on Discord), and how long a review usually
    takes. Then replace the sentence below. */}

Editors review when they can. If you have not heard anything after a
week, reply in your post in <Channel>get-involved</Channel>.

Once your article is live:

- Comms & Brand shares it in <Channel>announcements</Channel> and on our
  social media.
- Your name goes in the monthly recap.
- You can add it to your portal profile. Pick **Comms & Brand** as the
  team, and use the article's link.
  [Log your work on the portal](./log-your-work) shows how.

## If something goes wrong

**I can't see our blog in the list of blogs.** You have not been added
yet, or you have not accepted the invitation. Check Step 3. If you never
got an invitation, reply in your post in <Channel>get-involved</Channel>.

**Anything else?** Ask in <Channel>ask-for-help</Channel> on Discord. Say
what you tried and what you saw.
