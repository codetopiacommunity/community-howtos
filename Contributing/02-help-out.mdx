# Help out

You do not need a role or a team to help. When you have some time, pick
one of these.

---

## 1. Jump in when a team asks

Teams post what they need in <Channel>get-involved</Channel> on Discord. A flyer for
the next meetup, a hand testing the website, someone to take notes at a
session. No coding needed. Each post says what is needed, by when, and
how long it takes.

1. Open <Channel>get-involved</Channel>. It is a forum: each request is its own post
   with a title.
2. Find something you can do, and click or tap its title to open it.
3. Reply inside that post to say you will do it.
   **You will see:** the person who posted it replies that it is yours.
   Wait for that, so two people don't do the same thing.
4. Do it, and share it in the same post. To send a file or picture, use the
   **+** button next to the message box.

New to forums? The
[Using Discord](../getting-started/using-discord)
shows how, step by step.

The person who asked takes it from there.

One ask at a time, please. Life gets busy: if you can't finish, say so in
the post. If a post goes quiet for a week, the person who asked checks in,
and after two quiet weeks they offer it to someone else.

> [!TIP]
> Nothing there for you right now? Check back later. New asks come up
> every month, and more of them before events.

## 2. Volunteer at an event

When an event is coming up, the Events team posts a volunteer call in
<Channel>get-involved</Channel> with the **Volunteer call** tag. It is shared on our
socials too.

1. Open the volunteer call and click the form link in it.
2. Fill in the short form: your name, your portal username (the name
   after the `@` in your portal profile's address), the role you would
   like (check-in, setup, photos, timekeeping) and when you are free.
3. The Events lead picks volunteers and posts the plan in the volunteer
   call's thread a few days before.
4. Show up and do your part.

> [!NOTE]
> First time volunteering? You will be paired with someone who has done
> it before. You will not be left on your own.

## 3. Help someone who is stuck

Questions come into <Channel>ask-for-help</Channel> every day. If you know the answer,
or even part of it, open the question's post and reply inside it.

You do not need to be an expert. Someone a few steps behind you is
exactly who you can help. "I had the same error, this fixed it for me"
counts.

## 4. Fix something in our code

For people who code. Our projects keep their code tasks on GitHub as
<a href="https://github.com/codetopiacommunity/opensource-onboarding/blob/main/GLOSSARY.md#issue" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">issues</a> (a task written up on the project's page). New ones
appear in <Channel>github-help-wanted</Channel> on Discord.

Never made a <a href="https://github.com/codetopiacommunity/opensource-onboarding/blob/main/GLOSSARY.md#pull-request" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">pull request</a> (a proposed change)? Warm up
first with [Your first pull request, in your browser](./your-first-pull-request).
It takes about fifteen minutes.

1. Open <Channel>github-help-wanted</Channel> and click the link of a task
   that fits you. It opens on GitHub.
2. Read the whole task. It says what needs doing, where to look, when it
   is done, and the skills you need.
3. Look for **Assignees** (on the right, on a computer). **No one
   assigned** means the task is free. If a name is there, it is taken:
   pick another one.
4. Want it? Write a comment: `I'd like to take this up`.
5. Wait until a maintainer assigns you.
   **You will see:** your picture under **Assignees**. Now it is yours.
6. Not sure about something? Ask in the issue, as a comment. The answer
   then helps the next person too.
7. Do the work and open a pull request. In its description, write
   `Fixes #` and the issue's number, like `Fixes #12`.

A few things to know:

- **Labels.** **good first issue** is for your first real pull request. It
  runs without any secret keys. After that, pick **help wanted** tasks.
- **One task at a time,** until your pull request is merged (accepted).
- **Gone quiet?** After a week with no update, a maintainer checks in.
  After two quiet weeks, the task is freed for someone else. You can pick
  it up again later.
- **Nobody assigned you after a few days?** Ask in
  <Channel>ask-for-help</Channel> and paste the link to the issue.
- **Nothing free right now?** Open the project's **Pull requests** tab,
  read someone's change, and comment if something is unclear or you spot
  a problem. Or answer code questions in <Channel>ask-for-help</Channel>.
  Both help a lot.

## How you get recognised

You do not need to report anything. The leads keep track.

- **The monthly recap.** At the end of every month, everyone who helped
  is named, with what they did. It goes out in <Channel>announcements</Channel> on
  Discord and on our social media.
- **The Wall of Impact.** Standout work can get you a permanent spot on
  the <a href="https://community.codetopia.org/wall-of-impact" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Wall of Impact</a>,
  with your photo and what you did. A lead nominates you, and we ask
  you before anything is published.
- **Your portal profile.** If you like, you can add the work to your
  profile yourself, with a link to it.
  [Log your work on the portal](./log-your-work) shows how. Merged pull
  requests are added for you, once your GitHub username is on your
  profile.
