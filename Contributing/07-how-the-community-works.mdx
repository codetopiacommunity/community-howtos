# How the Community Works

You move up by doing, not by asking. Every level is visible, so anyone
can see how people got where they are.

Taking part is open at every level, including Member. The ladder only
tracks helping out and running things. If you are new to those words,
read [Ways to contribute](./06-ways-to-contribute) first.

---

## The ladder

| Level | How you get there | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| **Member** | Join: portal account and Discord | Events, channels, guides, opportunities |
| **Contributor** | Finish one thing that helped the community | The Contributor role on Discord and your name in the monthly recap |
| **Core** | Finish two things for a team, then accept a role from its lead | A named role, the team channel, real experience running things |
| **Lead** | Complete a core term well, then be appointed | Own a team, decide how it works, join the monthly leads meeting |
| **Alumni** | Finish a term and step back | Permanent credit, and you can come back without applying again |

🔗 [Help out](./04-make-your-first-contribution) is how you become a
Contributor.

## Getting a core role

You join a team by accepting a role, never by clicking a button.

1. **A lead posts an open role** on
   <a href="https://community.codetopia.org/careers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Careers</a>,
   marked **Core role · volunteer**, with a closing date. It says what
   you deliver, how much time it takes, and for how long.
2. **You apply by showing evidence.** Link to two things you have
   finished for that team: tasks from the board or your own ideas. No
   CV, no cover letter.
3. **You hear back:** yes, not yet and what is missing, or no and why.
   No word? Message the lead, then the Community Manager.
4. **On a yes,** the lead adds you to the team on the portal and gives
   you the Core role on Discord.

Sometimes a lead sends a listing to someone they have seen do good
work. It is the same listing and the same bar.

> [!NOTE]
> Experienced but new to the community? You still do two things for
> the team first. That takes a week or two, and it shows reliability,
> which a CV cannot.

**No open role for the team you want?** Tell the lead you are
interested. They will think of you when one opens. Keep helping out in
the meantime, because it all counts as evidence.

## Your first weeks in a core role

1. **In your first few days:** a 20-minute call with your lead. What
   "done" looks like this month, and who to ask for help.
2. **In your first week:** one small task you can finish in under 2
   hours, so you start with a win.
3. **After that:** a 3-line update in your team channel each week, and
   your role's monthly goal.

The weekly update is three lines:

```
Done: ___
Next: ___
Blocked: ___ (or "nothing")
```

That is the whole routine. No endless meetings.

## Terms, pausing and stepping back

Leaving is designed to be easy, so nobody has to disappear. You know
the exit rules before you say yes.

| Situation | What you do | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Your term ends** (3 months core, 6 months lead) | A 15-minute chat with your lead | Renew, switch roles, or step back. All three are fine |
| **Exams, a new job, family** | Tell your lead: "Pausing until [date], [name] covers" | Up to 6 weeks. Your role waits for you |
| **You want out mid-term** | Tell your lead | You are thanked publicly and move to Alumni. Hand over access and notes within a week |
| **You went quiet** | Nothing | After 6 weeks with no update, your lead checks in once, privately. No reply in 2 more weeks and the role reopens. You stay a member and can still help out |

Pausing and stepping back are one message, not a meeting. When you
step back, your lead asks one question: "What would have made you
stay?" Your answer helps us fix what is not working.

## How credit works

- **The monthly recap** names every contributor with what they did.
  "Ama designed the meetup flyer", never "thanks to the team".
- **Discord roles** match the ladder: Contributor, Core, Lead, Alumni.
- **A quarterly shout-out** for one person per team, chosen by the
  lead.
- **A reference letter or LinkedIn recommendation** from the Community
  Lead for anyone who completes a full core term well.
- **Visibility.** Core members host events, speak, and get introduced
  to partners.

Credit just happens. Once your contribution is confirmed, it goes in
the recap without you having to ask.

## When nothing happens

We are all volunteers, so we do not promise reply times. We do promise
that every wait has someone to nudge, and nudging is expected, not
rude.

| Waiting for | Nudge first | Then |
|---|---|---|
| An answer in Discord | Post again in the same thread | Tag the Community Manager |
| A finished task to be confirmed | The person who posted it, in the task thread | The team's lead |
| A pull request review | Comment on the pull request | Ask in the team's channel |
| A blog submission | Comms & Brand, in their channel | The Comms & Brand lead |
| A role application | The team's lead | The Community Manager |
